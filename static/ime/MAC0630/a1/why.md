The baseline mapping drives a 6-DoF task with a 2-DoF mouse (+1 DoF with mouse wheel) by decomposing it into two modes: translate and rotate. Inside rotate mode, mouse X maps to pitch, mouse Y to yaw, and the scroll wheel to roll, all simultaneously. This simultaneity is what I find to be the baseline's precision weakness, since a single hand motion couples three rotation axes, so correcting yaw unintentionally perturbs pitch and roll, and the participant chases the error. Our gizmo mapping decomposes one level further by constraining each drag to a single axis via raycast-selectable handles. Correcting one DoF cannot perturb the others, which I predict will reduce final position and orientation error. The hypothesis is that the gizmo trades a small speed cost for a significant precision gain.
The gizmo was designed based on good experiences using blender/unity to position objects in 3D space and based on the three.js [TransformControls implementation](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/controls/TransformControls.js).
There was some AI usage in the implementation of the gizmo, mainly to overcome some dificulties with Three.js syntax, but the core logic and design decisions are mine.
